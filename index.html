Working models

Four lenses for governing change

The models are not a methodology to follow mechanically. They are practical lenses for noticing what matters, making decisions visible and keeping effort proportionate.

Clarity and governance

Make the decision, evidence, risk and next action visible enough for people to act rather than merely approve.

Stakeholders moving together

Treat alignment as part of delivery, not a communications exercise added after the important decisions are made.

Safety to change

People adopt when the consequences of trying, questioning and correcting are survivable, not simply because they were trained.

Momentum and freedom

Use structure and automation to remove avoidable effort while preserving judgement, accountability and room to adapt.

The eMote approach

Change capability, not change theatre

Four propositions recur across the models, essays and tools.

Adoption is an outcome, not a hope

Change activity should concentrate on the real barriers to people using, trusting and sustaining the new way of working.

Governance should improve decisions

Boards and controls are useful when they expose assumptions, accountability and choices rather than create paperwork that simulates certainty.

Change work should be proportionate

Not every change needs a programme around it. The amount of change activity should follow impact, novelty, risk and capacity.

AI should remove avoidable effort

Use AI for drafting, synthesis and consistency where it helps, while keeping human judgement where consequences, context and accountability matter.
