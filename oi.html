Intelligence where work happens.

The opportunity

Make what you know
work for you.

The answer is often somewhere in the business. Getting it to the right person, with the authority to act, is the harder part.

Perhaps routine questions always reach the same expert. Staff move information between systems. Customers wait while a decision works its way up the organisation.

Organisational Intelligence starts with the outcome you want, then connects the knowledge, decisions and capabilities needed to achieve it.

AI can contribute by finding information, summarising context or supporting a judgement. Clearer responsibilities, better access to knowledge and simpler rules may matter just as much.

An illustrative SME example

A customer asks for help.
Can your team resolve it?

Consider a small service business where everyday enquiries depend on the owner being available.

The familiar pattern

Ask. Forward. Wait. Chase.

A customer asks whether a problem is covered. A colleague searches old emails, forwards the query to the owner and waits for a decision. The customer calls again.

A design to test

Understand. Decide. Resolve. Learn.

The colleague has access to current guidance, relevant history and clear decision limits. They resolve straightforward cases and escalate exceptions with the context already assembled.

People

Understand the customer's needs, exercise judgement and own the decision.

AI

Could retrieve relevant guidance and summarise the case for someone to check.

Automation

Could record the outcome, send an agreed update and trigger follow-up.

Success would be tested through resolution time, repeat contacts, decision quality and the owner's time. This is a design example, not a reported client result.

The eMote method

A method for turning understanding
into lasting change.

OI applies the eMote method to the way a business knows, decides, acts and learns.

E

Evidence

Find out what is happening. Use experience, observations and data to test assumptions and identify what needs to improve.

M

Model

Make sense of the business. Connect outcomes, decisions, knowledge and capabilities so you can explore a better design.

O

Operationalise

Make the design usable. Equip people, AI and automation with the knowledge, authority and boundaries needed to act.

T

Transform

Put the new way of working into practice. Test changes to roles, decisions and workflows against the outcomes that matter.

E

Embed

Make improvement sustainable. Support adoption, keep knowledge current and learn from results as the business changes.

These activities inform each other. An initial model helps identify the evidence you need; testing a change can reveal something that makes you rethink the model.

For OI, the focus is practical: bring knowledge and decision capability closer to where work happens, with clear accountability and feedback.

How the OI studio guides the work

The studio translates the method into six practical stages. These organise delivery; evidence, modelling and learning continue throughout, with governance built into every stage.

01

Understand

Identify the outcome, the people affected and how work and decisions happen today. Surface evidence and assumptions.

02

Redesign

Explore how knowledge, decision authority and support could move closer to the work.

03

Allocate

Decide what people, AI and automation should each do, with clear ownership and escalation.

04

Prove

Test a bounded improvement against agreed measures. Find out what works and what needs changing.

05

Govern

Make accountability, safeguards and review explicit. Build these into the design from the start.

06

Embed

Support adoption, maintain the knowledge and use feedback to improve how the business works.

The eMote method. A developing OI studio.

02 / Paid diagnostic

Understand the opportunity

Examine a bounded area of the business, its evidence, decisions and capabilities.

You leave with: practical priorities, proposed measures and a recommendation on whether a pilot is worthwhile.

Test an improvement

Design and test a specific change with the people doing the work, then review the results together.

Each step is agreed separately. The diagnostic should be useful even if you decide not to proceed to a pilot.
