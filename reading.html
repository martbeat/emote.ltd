Reading and influences

I am often asked what I read, follow, or return to when I am thinking about change, leadership, and the role of technology.

This page is not a list of recommended resources. It is a map of influence. Some of these sources are comfortable. Others are deliberately unsettling. All of them have shaped how I think about organisations as systems, how learning really happens, and why good intentions are rarely enough.

Start here

A way in, not a conclusion.

  • Harvard Business Review

    Widely shared thinking on leadership and management, useful as a common reference point even when it oversimplifies.

  • London Business School Think

    Research-led writing that treats leadership as a systemic and collective practice rather than a personal trait.

  • Nesta

    UK-focused innovation work that connects systems change, public value, and emerging technology.

  • Government Digital Service

    Practical guidance rooted in real delivery, showing what change looks like inside constrained public systems.

Go deeper

When the simple explanations stop working.

  • MIT Sloan Management Review

    Serious analysis of digital transformation, organisational learning, and why technology rarely delivers on its own.

  • Oxford Review

    Evidence-based management writing that challenges intuition, hype, and fashionable shortcuts.

  • Academy of Management Perspectives

    Translational theory explaining why rational change efforts so often fail in real organisations.

  • Institute for Government

    Clear-eyed analysis of how government actually works, including incentives, constraints, and trade-offs.

Challenge your assumptions

This may change how you see the problem.

  • Evidence-Based Change (Fielding Graduate University)

    Work in the Argyris and Schön tradition, focused on inquiry, defensive routines, and double-loop learning.

  • Santa Fe Institute

    Complexity science applied to organisations and systems, challenging linear planning assumptions.

  • AI Now Institute

    Critical research on AI, power, governance, and social impact that resists techno-neutral narratives.

  • Society for Organizational Learning

    Systems thinking in practice, including its tensions, failures, and political realities.

The order of these links changes from time to time. That is intentional. Learning is rarely linear, and neither is change.
